Why UNMTA Was Started

UNMTA was founded to bridge the critical gap between academic theory and industry demands. It serves as a collaborative sandbox where students interact, innovate, share ideas, and collaborate on real-world engineering and astrophysics challenges.

Through our platform, first-year students gain direct access to continuing students and a robust alumni network, receiving academic mentorship, career guidance, and insights into technical career paths.

THE CONSTITUTION OF UNMTA

University of Nairobi Microprocessors Technology Association
"Bridging Physics, Control Systems, and Space through Innovation"

Registered Association Code: UON/DOS/UNMTA/2026
Office of the Dean of Students, Chiromo Campus

Preamble

WE, THE STUDENTS of the University of Nairobi, Physics Department, pursuing Bachelor of Science in Microprocessor Technology & Instrumentation, and Bachelor of Science in Astronomy & Astrophysics:

RECOGNIZING the necessity of bridging the gap between rigorous academic physics principles and practical, real-world engineering, microcontrollers, control systems, and computational astrophysics application;

AWARE of the immense value of building a strong collaborative community that links first-year undergraduates, continuing scholars, experienced alumni, and corporate industry giants;

DETERMINED to foster teamwork, technical excellence, entrepreneurship, professional development, and community impact;

DO HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT, and bind ourselves to this Constitution of the University of Nairobi Microprocessors Technology Association (UNMTA) on this day.

Vision & Mission

Article 3.1: Vision Statement

To be the premier student-led technical association in the East African region, driving excellence in hardware prototyping, instrumentation design, applied space sciences, and professional readiness.

Article 3.2: Mission Statement

To foster a collaborative ecosystem for Physics Department students by providing hands-on hardware training, coding simulations, hackathons, expert-led mentorship, and industrial placement linkages, thereby equipping members to innovate globally.

Objectives of the Association

The primary core objectives of UNMTA are:

  1. Skill Development & Innovation: To promote hands-on microcontroller coding, embedded system programming, instrumentation controls, and astronomical data analysis.
  2. Practical Application: To assist members in applying theoretical physics and astrophysics models to physical builds.
  3. Research Support: To support members in conducting research, thesis preparation, and senior year capstone design projects.
  4. Leadership and Teamwork: To build administrative and project management skills through organizing hackathons, community events, and technical teams.
  5. Corporate & Professional Linkage: To establish and maintain connections with tech companies (such as Dell, Microsoft) and local institutions (such as the Nuclear Regulatory Authority) for internships and industrial visits.
  6. Mentorship & Peer Guidance: To connect first-year students with continuing upper-year students and alumni experts.
  7. Academic Growth: To organize tutoring cohorts, examination review sessions, and tech bootcamps.
  8. Entrepreneurship: To encourage members to commercialize technical builds and create hardware startups.

Membership Eligibility

Article 6.1: General Eligibility

Membership is open to all registered students of the University of Nairobi who demonstrate interest in applied science, instrumentation, space research, and microprocessor applications.

Article 6.2: Primary Target Members

The core constituency of the association comprises students residing at Chiromo Campus under the Department of Physics, specifically:

  • Bachelor of Science in Microprocessor Technology, Control and Instrumentation (formerly known as course I39).
  • Bachelor of Science in Astronomy and Astrophysics (formerly known as course I44).
  • Other physical science and engineering students meeting general interest criteria.

Article 6.3: Non-Discrimination

Any eligible student may join the association regardless of their year of study, race, gender, background, or religious affiliations, provided they comply with the terms of this constitution.

Membership Registration & Renewal

Article 7.1: Registration Fees

To obtain active membership status, an eligible student must complete the digital registration form and pay a non-refundable entry fee of KSh 200.

Article 7.2: Annual Renewal

To sustain membership rights, access resources, and vote in elections, members must pay a yearly renewal fee of KSh 100 at the commencement of each academic year.

Article 7.3: Activation of Status

A member's profile is declared "Active" only after registration, validation of credentials, and payment clearance. The administrator reserves the right to review the registration list.

Rights & Responsibilities of Members

Article 8.1: Rights of Members

Every active member has the right to:

  • Participate in all general meetings, workshops, hackathons, and social outings.
  • Utilize association resources, library tools, lab equipment, and access shared cloud repositories.
  • Vote in annual general elections and run for executive leadership positions (subject to criteria in Article 10).
  • Express feedback, suggest updates, and petition the executive committee.

Article 8.2: Responsibilities of Members

Every member is obligated to:

  • Maintain high levels of academic integrity and professional conduct at all university premises.
  • Participate actively in workshops and help mentor junior members.
  • Respect the directives of the elected executive committee and general association guidelines.
  • Timely pay annual renewal fees and registration charges.

Activities and Programs

The association shall run the following activities divided into two portfolios:

Portfolio A: Academic & Professional Programs

  • Hackathons: Coding and hardware sprints focused on embedded systems.
  • Webinars & Workshops: Industry expert talks focusing on microcontroller architectures, telemetry, and observatory telescopes.
  • Technical Talks & Conferences: Presenting research papers, space telemetry records, and control algorithms.
  • Industrial Visits: Tours to technical laboratories, Nuclear Regulatory authorities, space centers, and manufacturing units.
  • Mentorship: Structured alumni-to-student career coaching pipelines.

Portfolio B: Student Engagement Activities

  • orientations: Annual welcome events introducing first-years to the physics department resources.
  • Networking Events: Corporate dinners, tech networking sessions, and inter-association forums.
  • Community Outreach: Promoting STEM education in high schools and performing volunteer hardware maintenance.
  • Team-Building: Yearly sports days, board game tournaments, and social treks.

Leadership Structure

The executive committee shall be elected annually and comprise the following portfolios:

  1. Chairperson: The overall executive lead; represents the association in university administration, signs official correspondence, and oversees project execution.
  2. Vice Chairperson: Assists the Chairperson; directs internal committees, handles student welfare, and acts as president in the chair's absence.
  3. Secretary-General: Custodian of association records; handles minutes of meetings, calendars of events, and coordinates registration applications.
  4. Treasurer: Handles finance; collects registration fees, updates receipt ledgers, draft balance sheets, and reports financial standing.
  5. Organizing Secretary: Coordinates logistics, event venues, corporate permissions, and industrial visits.
  6. Social Media Manager: Administers the association's online brand, handles LinkedIn/Instagram postings, and design posters.
  7. Assistant Social Media Manager: Assists the manager, writes newsletters, and schedules online outreach events.
  8. Class Representatives: Elected reps representing Microprocessor (I39) and Astronomy (I44) cohorts across all four academic years.

Financial Management

Article 11.1: Sources of Funds

The association's finances shall be drawn from:

  • Registration fees (KSh 200) and Renewal fees (KSh 100).
  • Donations from university alumni, professors, and well-wishers.
  • Sponsorships from collaborating companies (Microsoft, Dell, etc.).
  • Grants for innovation research projects and student hackathons.

Article 11.2: Transparency and Reports

The Treasurer must present a audited financial report during the Annual General Meeting (AGM) and make all financial accounts open to inspection by the executive committee and university auditor.

Meetings

Article 12.1: Regular Meetings

The general body shall meet at least three times every academic semester. Technical workshops and project groups shall meet weekly or biweekly as determined by group captains.

Article 12.2: Quorum

A quorum for any constitutional amendment or election voting shall consist of at least two-thirds (2/3) of all active registered members of the association.

Article 12.3: Apology and Absences

Executive committee members who are unable to attend scheduled meetings must issue a written apology to the Secretary-General at least 24 hours prior.

Discipline and Conduct

Article 13.1: Misconduct

Any member who acts in a manner that damages the reputation of the association, misappropriates funds, violates university codes, or behaves unprofessionally will face disciplinary action.

Article 13.2: Disciplinary Procedures

Disciplinary actions will be conducted by an independent committee appointed by the Vice Chairperson, comprising: 1. A warning letter detailing the infraction. 2. A suspension period (up to one semester) for repeating offenses. 3. Permanent termination of membership for financial fraud, theft, or severe misconduct.

Amendments

Any clause of this constitution may be reviewed, amended, or repealed subject to:

  1. A written proposal signed by at least 15 active members submitted to the Secretary-General.
  2. Discussion of the proposal during a Special General Meeting.
  3. Approval by a two-thirds (2/3) majority vote of registered active members attending the general voting.

Dissolution Clause

The association may only be dissolved if supported by a 90% vote of all registered members. In the event of dissolution, all equipment, assets, and balances shall be transferred to the University of Nairobi Physics Department laboratory to support future technical students.
